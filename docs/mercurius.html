What it is

Surgery, not pretraining

Mercurius is a family of models derived from Qwen3.5 by editing the architecture of the trained weights, then adapting them back to health. Nothing is trained from scratch. The bet is that the expensive part of a good model is the knowledge already in it, and the cheap part is the shape it is served in — so you can change the shape and buy back the quality for a fraction of what pretraining costs.

Every number on this page was produced on a single Jetson AGX Orin with 64 GB of unified memory. That constraint is the point. If a conversion pipeline only works on a pod of H100s, it is not a pipeline a small lab can run.

Sizes are named by parameter count — Mercurius 0.8B, Mercurius 9B — or S/M/L/XL where a rough tier is more useful. Only the 0.8B has been measured; everything below refers to it.

Where the KV cache lives

Six layers out of twenty-four

Qwen3.5 is already a hybrid: eight repeats of three GatedDeltaNet (linear-attention) blocks followed by one full GatedAttention block. Only those six full-attention layers hold a KV cache at all — at indices 3, 7, 11, 15, 19 and 23.

18 × GatedDeltaNet — linear, no cache 6 × GatedAttention — full, KV-cached

This bounds the prize honestly. The hybrid has already removed most of the cache a dense 24-layer transformer would carry, so compressing what remains is architectural alignment more than a dramatic memory win. We report it as such.

The pipeline

Four stages, and what each one cost

Each stage runs on the output of the last, at full precision, before any quantization. Stages are gated: a conversion that claims to be exact has to prove it against a matched perturbation control before anything downstream is measured.

A · B

Norm fusion, then GatedDeltaNet → KDA

RMSNorm gains are folded into the weights of every consumer projection, and the linear-attention blocks are lifted to Kimi Delta Attention by tiling the scalar decay parameter into a diagonal — the tiled initialization reproduces the original mapping exactly, so the richer parameterization starts as a no-op.

bit-exact verified against the unmodified model
C

NoPE — every RoPE frequency identity-ized

Rotary position encoding is switched off by driving all frequencies to identity, which changes no weights. The thesis is that the KDA layers carry position well enough on their own. It is the one stage that costs real quality up front.

+12.70% ppl at surgery → +0.65% after 2.46M tokens
D

TransMLA — a shared low-rank KV latent

K and V on the six attention layers are replaced by one joint latent, balanced before factorization so the rank budget is not spent on whichever matrix is larger. The factorization is done in the whitened basis (CARE), which optimizes activation error rather than weight error.

4.00× KV for +8.40% before recovery, +4.8% after
Stage D in detail

Whitening changes what compression costs

Plain SVD minimizes ‖W − Ŵ‖. That is the wrong objective — what the model experiences is ‖XW − XŴ‖. Whitening by the calibration covariance before truncating optimizes the error the model actually sees. The gap between the two is small at mild compression and enormous at aggressive compression.

Read naively, the compressed model looks better than baseline at 8k (−2.15%). It is not. The control reached −6.64% with no compression at all, so the true cost of 4× KV at matched training is about +4.8%. Recovery does real work — untrained the cost is +8.40% at 2k and +17.10% at 8k — but it plateaus near +5% rather than closing.

The delta is also far steadier than either arm alone. Each absolute number swings by several points between checkpoints while the difference stays within 4.69–5.67%, so whatever drives the swing — learning-rate schedule, drift toward the training distribution — affects both arms equally and cancels. The earlier "roughly free" reading was an artifact of comparing against a baseline that never received the same training.

That ~5% perplexity figure is robust. It does not move across LoRA versus dense training, 12M versus 294M trainable parameters, 150 versus 400 steps, or 1M versus 5M cached distillation tokens. Four independent attempts to spend it down land in the same band, which reads as an information floor at this rank rather than a training shortfall.

Perplexity understates this badly

Quoting ~5% as the cost of 4× KV would be misleading, and we did so in an earlier version of this page. Measured against the same matched control, retrieval degrades far faster — and the gap grows with distance.

At a 16k gap the compression costs 30.4% of retrieval — six times its perplexity cost, and the degradation is monotonic in distance. In hindsight the mechanism is obvious: the KV cache is the long-range retrieval path, so quartering its rank destroys distant-key discrimination while barely touching next-token prediction, which is dominated by local context. Perplexity was structurally the wrong instrument for this stage.

The honest summary: 4× KV cache costs about 5% perplexity and about 30% of long-range retrieval. If your workload is generation over short contexts, the first number is the relevant one. If it involves retrieving from far back in a long context, the second one is, and 4× is probably too aggressive — the CARE table above shows 2.00× at +0.42% perplexity before any recovery, which is where a retrieval-sensitive deployment should start.

Honest failures

What is still broken

NoPE fails on late-placed needles — not on long ones

At 131k tokens with the needle at depth 0.9, NoPE scores a retrieval gain of 4.809 against 11.237 for the RoPE-keeping control — 43% of the control, with untrained NoPE at 0.188. The obvious reading is that NoPE runs out of range, and the full depth×length grid says that reading is wrong. Because the scored copy sits at the end of the sequence, depth sets the gap between the two copies: depth 0.9 is a 13k lookback, depth 0.1 a 118k one. At depth 0.1 NoPE reaches 1.011–1.090× the control across 32k, 65k and 131k — parity or better at the longest gaps. The deficit is confined to needles placed late in the context, which points at a missing recency signal rather than exhausted range, and changes which fixes are worth trying.

A third of the NoPE headline was not NoPE

The +12.70% → +0.65% recovery looks better than it is. A matched control arm that kept RoPE and trained on the same data closed roughly a third of that distance on its own. The honest reading is that NoPE recovery is real but two-thirds the size of the raw before-and-after, and we would not have known without running the control.

Vision is untested

The vision tower is excluded from every measurement here. It was left out deliberately to keep the first pass tractable, and its capabilities may well have degraded. Nothing on this page should be read as a claim about multimodal behavior.

Initialization dominates training

Three results worth carrying forward: seeding the KDA decay gates moves them about 30× further than the entire training run does; a decay target of α ≈ 0.60 outperformed the 0.47 taken from prior work; and initializing the gates to exactly reproduce GatedDeltaNet — the intuitively safest choice — was the worst configuration for long context. Where you start matters more than how long you train.

Reproduce

The whole pipeline, one device

Every stage is gated and every gate is measured against a control. Running these in order rebuilds the model on this page.

# fuse norms, lift GatedDeltaNet → KDA — asserts bit-exactness
python src/build_stage_ab.py

# calibrate activation covariances, compare plain SVD against whitened
python src/run_care.py

# latent-KV sweep, with a full-rank no-op check before any lossy rank
python src/run_stage_d.py

# save the calibration covariances the whitened factorization needs
python src/save_covs.py

# recovery: NoPE + whitened latent KV at 4× cache, distilled against
# a cached top-k teacher. --mla-covs is required, not optional: without
# it the run refuses outright rather than quietly falling back to the
# plain-SVD factorization, which is far worse at this rank.
python src/train_recovery.py --dial nope --mla-dc 256 \
    --mla-covs cache/kv_covs.pt \
    --logit-cache cache/tk64_1m.pt --topk 64 --length-mix

# needle-in-a-haystack across depth × length
python src/eval_longctx.py

Training is distilled against a pre-computed top-k logit cache, so the teacher never runs during the loop — which is what makes recovery affordable on one device. Sequence lengths are sampled from a mixture rather than fixed, putting long-context gradient signal into the run at about a fifth of the cost of training at full length throughout.

Roadmap

What comes next

  • Done

    Recovery on the compressed model, with a control. 4× KV costs about +5% perplexity at matched training — roughly half the untrained damage at 2k and a quarter of it at 8k, but it plateaus rather than closing.

  • Next

    Retrieval on the compressed model. Every number here is perplexity. The depth×length grid showed perplexity and retrieval moving independently, so the compression result should not be assumed to transfer to needle retrieval until measured.

  • Next

    Long-range training data. Targeted repeated-span data aimed squarely at the depth-0.9 retrieval failure, plus multi-needle and multi-seed evaluation for statistical power.

  • Planned

    Mercurius 9B. Testing whether the pipeline transfers up a size class unchanged, which is the claim that makes it a family rather than a one-off.

  • Deferred

    Vision. Re-attaching and measuring the vision tower once the language-side pipeline is settled.

Mercurius is the Roman god of translation and of boundaries — of carrying something across into a new form, and of doing it quickly. These models are Qwen3.5 carried across into a cheaper architecture, not built from nothing. The family name is the only mythological one; sizes are parameter counts.
